      Where does the sun fall on your lot?

      1. Type your address anywhere in Metro Vancouver.
      2. See your lot in 3D, with its real neighbours, trees and buildings, built from laser scans of the city.
      3. Find the sunny and shady spots: hours of direct sun for each spot, right now, on any day, or over the growing season.

      How to read this

      • Colours: yellow spots get the most direct sun, dark blue the least.
      • Hatched spots are under a roof or tree.
      • Move around: drag to turn the view, scroll to zoom, right-drag to slide.
      • Move around: drag sideways to turn the view, pinch to zoom, two fingers to tilt. Scroll the page as usual.
      • Click any spot on the lot to see its sun month by month.

        About VanShade and its accuracy

        VanShade works out where direct sunlight falls on a Metro Vancouver lot. It finds the lot from your address, loads 1 m LiDAR elevation for the lot and 200 m around it (roofs, neighbouring buildings, trees and hedges included), and for every square metre of the lot works out which parts of the sky are blocked. It then looks for sharper and newer LiDAR: by default it keeps the 0.5 m detail of NRCan's point clouds wherever the Province's 2024–2025 surveys show nothing changed, and uses the newer survey wherever something did. "Elevation data" lets you pick either survey on its own. It then follows the sun across the day or season to count hours of direct sun. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere except the map data requests.

        What to keep in mind

        • Lot lines are approximate. They come from ParcelMap BC, which is not a legal survey.
        • The LiDAR has a date. Most of Metro Vancouver was flown in 2016. New buildings, removed trees and years of growth since then aren't in it.
        • Trees are treated as solid all year. Deciduous trees let much more winter light through than shown.
        • This is potential direct sun. It assumes clear skies; cloud and haze aren't counted. Bright shade and reflected light aren't counted either.
        • Distant hills aren't included. Only things within about 200 m of the lot can cast shadows, so on the North Shore and other hillsides, winter mornings and evenings may be shadier than shown.
        • Sun position comes from SunCalc, including the bending of light low near the horizon. Times are Vancouver time.
        • Where you stand matters. "Garden bed" measures 0.3 m above the ground, "Seated" 1.2 m, and "Rooftop" just above the roof or deck surface. Spots under a roof or tree canopy are hatched; at ground level they're mostly in shade.
        • Big lots are coarser. Above about 20,000 m², sun is worked out on a 2 m (or coarser) grid.
